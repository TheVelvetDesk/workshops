AI governance & responsible use

Useful AI requires judgment, boundaries and accountability.

This policy sets the operating standard for how The Velvet Desk teaches, evaluates and uses AI. It applies to workshops, workflow reviews, demonstrations, recommendations and internal AI-assisted work.

1. Scope and accountability

This policy applies to The Velvet Desk personnel, instructors, contractors and approved tools used for curriculum development, workshop demonstrations, workflow assessments, creative production and administration.

The Velvet Desk owns this policy. The person delivering a workshop or recommendation is accountable for the use of AI, the quality of the output and escalation of privacy, safety or legal concerns. No tool is treated as the accountable decision-maker.

2. Human oversight and decisions

  • Questionnaire responses may be organized or summarized with AI assistance, but a person reviews the application and determines the response.
  • We do not use AI to make final decisions concerning employment, housing, credit, insurance, health care, education access or another legally significant opportunity.
  • High-impact recommendations require appropriate professional, legal, financial, medical or security review.
  • Participants are taught when a task should remain human-led and where review checkpoints belong.

3. Data governance and privacy

  • Minimize: use the least data needed for the learning or business objective.
  • Sanitize: remove names, account numbers, customer records, confidential terms and unnecessary identifiers from examples.
  • Classify: treat health, financial, legal, employee, biometric, precise-location and government-identifier data as high risk.
  • Restrict: do not place passwords, payment details or regulated records into consumer AI tools.
  • Retain deliberately: archive or delete application records when they are no longer reasonably needed.
  • No public-model training: we do not intentionally use identifiable questionnaire responses to train or fine-tune a public or general-purpose AI model.

Our detailed collection and request practices are in the Privacy Notice.

4. Permitted and prohibited uses

Permitted with appropriate review

Drafting, summarization, research support, ideation, coding assistance, workflow mapping, content creation, customer-service preparation and administrative support may be used when the input is appropriate and the output is reviewed.

Not permitted

  • Deceptive impersonation, fraud, harassment, illegal surveillance or evasion of law.
  • Using sensitive traits to discriminate, exploit or make unsupported high-impact decisions.
  • Uploading confidential, copyrighted or personal material without authority and a valid purpose.
  • Presenting unverified AI output as professional, legal, medical, financial or safety advice.
  • Deploying an automated customer-facing system without defined escalation, monitoring and a responsible owner.

5. Accuracy, testing and transparency

AI output can be incomplete, fabricated, biased or outdated. Material claims should be checked against authoritative sources, and important workflows should be tested with representative examples, failure cases and human review.

When AI-generated text, images, audio or video could reasonably mislead someone about authenticity, authorship or a real person, we teach appropriate disclosure, labeling and platform-specific provenance practices. We do not remove provenance signals for deceptive purposes.

6. Fairness and accessibility

We evaluate whether a proposed AI use could produce unequal outcomes, exclude people with disabilities or rely on proxies for protected characteristics. Where risk is meaningful, we limit the use, add human review, test across relevant groups or recommend that AI not be used.

Workshop materials should use plain language, accessible formats and beginner-friendly alternatives whenever practical.

7. Intellectual property and content rights

Participants should use content they own, license or are authorized to process. AI output must be reviewed for confidential information, trademarks, copyrighted expression, publicity rights and platform restrictions before commercial use. AI output is not assumed to be exclusive or automatically protectable.

8. AI vendor governance

Before recommending or using a tool for a meaningful business workflow, we consider its purpose, data practices, account controls, training settings, retention, security, output limitations, integration permissions and exit options. Higher-risk data requires stronger contractual and technical safeguards than a public demonstration.

Vendor approval is use-case-specific. A tool that is appropriate for public marketing drafts may not be appropriate for employee, customer, financial or health information.

9. Incident and concern response

If we identify unintended disclosure, harmful output, unauthorized access, a deceptive use or a material policy violation, we will take reasonable steps to contain the issue, preserve relevant facts, suspend the affected use when appropriate, notify responsible parties and improve the control that failed. Legally required notifications will be handled according to applicable law.

10. California legal and regulatory framework

We monitor California requirements according to the role we actually perform:

  • CalOPPA: because the site collects personally identifiable information from Californians, we conspicuously provide a privacy notice describing collection, disclosures, user choices, changes and browser-tracking practices.
  • CCPA/CPRA: applicability depends on statutory business thresholds and processing. We follow core principles of notice, minimization, proportionality, reasonable security and practical access, correction and deletion even where a particular statutory duty may not apply.
  • Automated decisionmaking: California’s finalized ADMT regulations became effective January 1, 2026, with ADMT-specific compliance beginning January 1, 2027 for covered significant decisions. Our questionnaires are human-reviewed and are not used to make covered significant decisions.
  • Generative AI training transparency: AB 2013 requires developers making covered generative-AI systems publicly available to Californians to publish training-data documentation. The Velvet Desk currently teaches and uses third-party AI tools; it does not currently train or substantially modify a public generative-AI model. We will reassess if that changes.
Scope matters: Publishing this policy is an operational commitment, not a claim that every AI statute applies to every workshop or that a regulator has certified our practices.

Official references: California DOJ privacy guidance · CPPA privacy FAQ · Final ADMT and risk regulations · AB 2013

11. Review and training

We review this policy at least annually and sooner when our services, vendors, risk profile or applicable law materially changes. Training and workshop materials are updated when a control or legal development changes the recommended practice.

12. Questions and concerns

Report an AI, privacy or safety concern to thevelvetdeskadvisory@gmail.com. Include enough information to identify the relevant workshop or workflow, but do not email passwords or sensitive records.

This policy is an internal operating standard and educational statement. It is not legal advice or a substitute for advice from qualified counsel about a specific organization or use case.
