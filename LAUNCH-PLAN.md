# AI Workbench San Diego — pilot launch plan

Updated August 22, 2026.

## The recommendation

Do not lead with “AI classes for boomers.” Lead with a business problem and use age-neutral, beginner-friendly language.

The best first customer is an owner-operator or operations/office manager at a 2–25 person service business who:

- personally handles recurring email, estimates, customer follow-up, reviews, documents, research, or marketing;
- loses at least two hours a week to one repeatable task;
- has authority to change the process;
- works mostly with digital inputs; and
- wants help but does not want a technical course.

Good first verticals include home services, property management and real estate, consultants and agencies, bookkeeping firms, and non-clinical wellness businesses. Avoid regulated or highly sensitive workflows in the pilot.

Baby-boomer owners are a useful audience within this group, but age should be a secondary attribute, not the headline. The marketing language should say “patient, beginner-friendly, no jargon, no coding.” A later partner event can be called “Practical AI for Established Business Owners” without mentioning age.

## Why demand is plausible

There is a clear training gap, but also substantial free competition:

- The [NFIB 2025 Small Business and Technology survey](https://www.nfib.com/news/press-release/new-nfib-report-how-small-businesses-incorporate-tech-and-ai-advancements/) found 24% of small employers using AI, while 63% expected it to matter to their industry within five years. Communication and marketing were the most common planned uses.
- The [U.S. Chamber’s 2025 report](https://www.uschamber.com/technology/empowering-small-business-the-impact-of-technology-on-u-s-small-business) reported 58% generative-AI use in its sample; a later [training guide](https://www.uschamber.com/co/start/strategy/ai-training-guide-small-business) says more than 70% of small-business owners would benefit from more implementation training. The adoption numbers differ because the surveys use different samples and definitions, but both show interest plus incomplete implementation.
- San Diego has hosted a free [OpenAI Academy small-business workshop](https://academy.openai.com/public/events/chatgpt-for-small-businesses-san-diego-pa4ddefxcd), and the San Diego & Imperial SBDC lists free AI programming. This validates the topic while making “AI basics” alone hard to charge for.
- A local provider advertises hands-on workshops [starting around $50–$100 per person](https://www.peacefulaisolutions.com/training), and a prior focused local workshop was [priced at $75](https://luma.com/anub2l85).

The paid advantage therefore has to be: five seats, individualized preparation, the participant’s real workflow, tangible take-home assets, and accountability after the event.

## Pricing

### Public pilot

- $149 total for two hours
- $49 seat deposit after fit and date confirmation
- $100 balance due 72 hours before the event
- Minimum four paid seats; maximum five
- Founding participants provide a short survey and permission to request a testimonial (permission is not required to attend)

At five seats, gross revenue is $745. A room at roughly $40–$60 per hour for 2.5 hours, light refreshments, and payment fees should leave about $500–$550 before instructor preparation and taxes. Current examples include a [six-person Hera Hub room at $40/hour](https://www.peerspace.com/pages/listings/64085866f9d022000eb7f5c2), [SmartSpace rooms at $60/hour](https://smartspacesd.com/book-office/), and a [six-person Moniker Commons room at $60/hour](https://www.monikercommons.com/conference-rooms). Confirm live availability and total booking fees before advertising the venue.

### After the first two cohorts

- Standard public workshop: $199 per person
- Premium workflow clinic: $299 per person, including a 20-minute pre-call and 30-minute follow-up
- Private team workshop: start at $1,250 for up to eight people, with a scoped company use case

Charging $150 per hour per person ($300 total) is reasonable only for the premium clinic. It is too aggressive for an unproven, cold-audience beginner pilot in a market with free and $50–$100 alternatives.

## Curriculum ladder

Do not mix beginner through advanced students in one room. Keep each workshop organized around one outcome.

### Level 1 — working foundation

**AI for Small-Business Owners: Save Five Hours a Week**

- select a safe, useful task;
- give AI context and constraints;
- map human and AI steps;
- build and test a reusable prompt;
- create a review checklist; and
- estimate time saved and choose the next task.

### Level 2 — role-specific clinics

1. **Inbox to Action:** draft replies, summarize threads, and create follow-up lists.
2. **The One-Person Marketing System:** turn one idea into a useful email, post, and FAQ without generic copy.
3. **SOP Builder:** turn notes or a screen-recording transcript into a staff-ready procedure.
4. **Faster Estimates and Proposals:** organize discovery notes into a consistent first draft with human checks.
5. **Customer Voice Lab:** summarize reviews and feedback into themes, FAQs, and service improvements.

### Level 3 — automation readiness

**From Prompt to Process:** identify triggers, handoffs, approvals, failure cases, data boundaries, and whether a no-code automation is worth building. This is where Zapier, Make, or custom tools can be introduced; they should not dominate the first workshop.

## The 120-minute pilot agenda

| Time | Activity | Participant output |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 | Task scoring and data boundary | One task to use; one task not to use |
| 10–30 | Context, constraints, examples, output format | First structured brief |
| 30–50 | Human/AI workflow map | Current and proposed steps |
| 50–95 | Guided build on each laptop | Reusable prompt, input list, output template |
| 95–110 | Second-example test and quality review | Revised workflow and review checklist |
| 110–120 | Measure and plan | Weekly time estimate and next opportunity |

## Application design

The site’s event-specific application intentionally asks only what changes the workshop:

- name, business, role, industry, and team size;
- the recurring task and how often it happens;
- estimated minutes per occurrence;
- current AI comfort;
- desired result;
- schedule windows;
- tools already used; and
- accessibility/learning needs plus a safe-data acknowledgement.

Do not ask applicants to upload real business files before the session. After payment, send a separate pre-work email explaining how to sanitize a sample.

## Where to list and market

### 1. Eventbrite as the main public listing

Eventbrite currently lets organizers publish without an organizer subscription and charges paid-ticket fees. Its listed U.S. pricing is [3.7% + $1.79 per paid ticket plus 2.9% payment processing per order](https://www.eventbrite.com/organizer/pricing/). Use Eventbrite for discovery, reminders, and social proof, but point “Learn more / apply” to the event-specific website when platform rules allow. Avoid duplicate checkout paths: deposits should be collected in one system only.

### 2. Keep Meetup for a measured 60-day test

Meetup is not automatically a bad deal. Official plans [start at $29.99 per month and vary by account/location](https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/28677808413197-Organizer-subscription-prices-overview). At the quoted $40, one incremental $149 customer pays for more than three months. Add a unique `meetup` source link or ask “How did you hear about us?” Keep it only if it produces at least one qualified applicant or useful partner introduction in 60 days.

Use Meetup for community and recurring free/low-cost previews, not as the sole checkout or customer database.

### 3. Borrow trust from local partners

Ask coworking spaces to co-host in exchange for venue visibility or a referral fee. Contact neighborhood business associations, chambers, industry associations, and trusted small-business advisers with a concrete offer: a free 30-minute demonstration for their members followed by an optional five-seat clinic.

SBDC and SCORE already provide free education, so do not pitch a generic competing lecture. Offer the tiny individualized lab they cannot easily provide at scale. The [City of San Diego business-support page](https://www.sandiego.gov/economic-development/business) is a useful map of the local support ecosystem.

### 4. Founder-led LinkedIn outreach

Post a 45–60 second before/after demonstration of one workflow. Invite local owners and office managers personally, with no mass-sales language. Create a LinkedIn Event and ask coworking/association partners to reshare it.

### 5. Secondary channels

- Facebook and Nextdoor groups for neighborhood businesses
- relevant local Instagram accounts
- past clients, friends, accountants, bookkeepers, and web professionals who already advise small businesses
- Luma for simple RSVP pages only if it adds reach; keep the website as the canonical event page

Do not buy broad Meta or Google ads until the offer converts from partner and founder-led traffic.

## Listing copy

### Title

AI for Small-Business Owners: Save Five Hours a Week — Hands-on San Diego Workshop

### Short description

Bring one repetitive business task and leave two hours later with a tested, reusable AI workflow. Five seats, beginner-friendly, no coding, and no generic lecture.

### What makes it different

You will work on a sanitized example from your own business. By the end, you will have a reusable prompt, input checklist, tested output, human-review checklist, time-saved estimate, one task that should not use AI, and your next automation opportunity.

### What to bring

A charged laptop, access to an AI assistant, and a non-sensitive example of one recurring task. Do not bring passwords or unredacted customer, employee, health, legal, or payment data.

## 30-day validation test

### Week 1

- Publish the landing page and the Eventbrite listing.
- Keep the Meetup listing but add a source-specific link.
- Contact ten coworking/community partners and 25 local owners or office managers.

### Week 2

- Run one free 30-minute live demonstration online or at the likely venue.
- Post two short workflow demonstrations on LinkedIn.
- Interview five applicants for ten minutes each; listen for exact tasks and willingness to pay.

### Week 3

- Match a date only when at least four qualified applicants share a window.
- Send the $49 deposit link with a 48-hour deadline.
- Reserve the room after four deposits clear and the venue cancellation terms are acceptable.

### Week 4

- Run the pilot.
- Collect a before/after time estimate and a five-question feedback survey.
- Ask for a specific testimonial and one referral.
- Publish the next outcome-specific workshop within 48 hours.

### Go/no-go metrics

- 10 qualified applications in 30 days
- 4 deposits from one compatible schedule window
- at least 3 attendees complete a reusable workflow
- average usefulness rating of 8/10 or higher
- at least 2 referrals, testimonials, or next-workshop commitments
- tracked source for every applicant

If applications are high but deposits are low, test $99 once before changing the audience. If applications are low, change the niche/title/channel before changing the curriculum. If attendees want the work done for them, that is a consulting lead, not a reason to turn the group workshop into implementation work.
